IN PLAIN WORDS

Every night, survey telescopes flag possible new near-Earth asteroids and post them to the NEO Confirmation Page. Astronomers must observe them again within days, or their orbits become too uncertain and they are lost. Only about half turn out to be real; the rest are camera artifacts, satellites, stars or ordinary main-belt asteroids.

The ranking puts the likeliest real ones first, so scarce telescope time goes where it matters. P(real NEO) is the chance a candidate is a genuine near-Earth object, not the chance of hitting Earth. No candidate here has an impact assessment; known impact risks are in NASA's Sentry table further down.

EVALUATION · ON CANDIDATES IT NEVER SAW

Is the model any good?

Yes, at ranking: on candidates it had never seen, posted after it was trained, it ranked real near-Earth objects above junk better than the digest2 score alone (ROC-AUC 0.93 vs 0.70). At a cut-off that keeps 95% of real ones, it threw out 65% of the junk; digest2 alone threw out 37%.
