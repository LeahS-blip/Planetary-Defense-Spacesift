IN PLAIN WORDS

Every night, survey telescopes flag possible new near-Earth asteroids and post them to the NEO Confirmation Page. Astronomers must observe them again within days, or their orbits become too uncertain and they are lost. Only about half turn out to be real; the rest are camera artifacts, satellites, stars or ordinary main-belt asteroids.

The ranking puts the likeliest real ones first, so scarce telescope time goes where it matters. P(real NEO) is the chance a candidate is a genuine near-Earth object, not the chance of hitting Earth. No candidate here has an impact assessment; known impact risks are in NASA's Sentry table further down.

BEFORE YOU ACT ON THIS RANKING

  • It ranks better than the digest2 score alone (ROC-AUC 0.90 vs 0.67, tested on 403 candidates posted after 2026-10-04, trained on 1208 earlier ones), but at the strict setting that keeps ≥99% of real NEOs it drops only 26% of junk (digest2 alone: 26%).
  • Probabilities are not calibrated: use them to order follow-up, not as odds.
  • Labels are noisy: “real” means later designated a near-Earth object; everything else, including candidates nobody followed up (some of them real), counts as not real. That bias affects both the model and digest2.
  • Not a head-to-head: the model uses digest2's scores plus extra context (observatory, brightness, sky position, motion).
  • Trained on 1611 candidates (last retrained 2 Nov 2026; retrained every Monday); unusual candidates unlike past ones may be scored poorly.
  • Not an impact forecast: a high score means “probably a real near-Earth object”, nothing about hitting Earth.

EVALUATION · ON CANDIDATES IT NEVER SAW

Is the model any good?

Yes, at ranking: on candidates it had never seen, posted after it was trained, it ranked real near-Earth objects above junk better than the digest2 score alone (ROC-AUC 0.90 vs 0.67). At a cut-off that keeps 95% of real ones, it threw out 61% of the junk; digest2 alone threw out 39%.
Tested on 403 candidates (218 real) over 28 days; 95% intervals 0.87–0.93 vs 0.62–0.72. An early result: it is retrained and retested every Monday, and the numbers will move. The model sees digest2's scores plus context digest2 doesn't use (observatory, brightness, sky position, motion), so this measures whether that context helps, not a head-to-head of equals.
